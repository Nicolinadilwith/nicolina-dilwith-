# Bio

I own and operate a small dog daycare business on Oahu. I've been training dogs for almost ten years — that experience predates the daycare and still shapes how it's run day to day — and I built the business around keeping real, personal relationships with dog owners, so it feels like a community rather than a transaction.

The business almost didn't survive its first real test. When lockdowns hit in March 2020, daycare had little value to a dog whose owner was suddenly home all day, and I kept the doors open by discounting services for essential workers. As "covid puppies" matured and their owners returned to offices, separation anxiety created a wave of sudden demand, and the business grew fast through 2022 and 2023. That boom has since leveled off, which is exactly the kind of question I now bring into this coursework: how to price and position a mature business in a market that's stopped expanding, rather than one still running on survival-mode decisions.

My long-term goal is to keep growing the business while holding onto the same quality of care and the same personal relationships with clients that it was built on — not trading one for the other as it scales.
